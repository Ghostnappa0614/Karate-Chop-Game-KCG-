Karate Chop Game

Like tag — but speed is replaced by accuracy and restraint. The goal is not to hurt; it's to land clean.

How to join Read the rules

Once you're in, you're in. There is no quitting KCG.

The concept

A sport you play anywhere, forever.

KCG is a continuous, turn-based contact sport played between two or more players, anywhere, anytime. It blends martial arts precision with a social, honor-based game system — like tag, but speed is replaced by accuracy and restraint.

The point

The goal is not to hurt; it's to land clean.

Joining the game

You chop your way in.

  1. A new player must be read the full rules before entering.
  2. To officially join, they must "chop into the game" — landing a legal chop on any currently active player.
  3. Once in, you are in. There is no quitting KCG. This is a core, non-negotiable part of the game's identity.

What counts as a chop

A "chop" is defined as a clean, flat-handed, karate-style strike using the blade of the hand. No fists, no grabs, no swinging strikes.

Turn system

Order of play.

  • Whoever chops first sets the turn order.
  • There is no limit on chopping the same person. Players can chop each other unlimited times, back to back.
  • Location reset rule: once all players leave a shared location, the chop queue resets and turn order starts fresh.
  • Turns continue indefinitely as long as players keep chopping each other in sequence.

Blocking

A block redirects — it doesn't end.

  • All chops — except penalty chops — can be blocked.
  • A successful block does not spend the chopper's turn obligation. Instead, the turn transfers to the blocker. The blocker becomes the active chopper and must continue the chop chain.
  • Blocks must be arm-to-arm deflections. Catching, grabbing, or trapping the striking arm is not a valid block.
Practical effect

A block does not end an exchange — it redirects it. Blocking is a tactical choice, since the blocker takes on the obligation to chop next rather than simply avoiding a hit.

Penalty chops

Fouls and what they cost.

Penalty chops are unblockable. The recipient of the foul chooses where on the offender's body — within legal zones — the penalty lands.

Fouls and penalties

FoulPenalty
Hitting a banned zone Disqualification from the round
Chopping someone who is driving 1 penalty chop
Chopping someone mid-meal or holding an open drink 1 penalty chop
Chopping a non-player (someone not in the game) Every current, present, and active member lines up and chops the offender one at a time
Malicious intent — excessive force, anger, aggression Disqualification from the current session

The spirit of the game

Precision, timing and humor — not pain.

All chops must be delivered with zero malicious intent — firm and clear, never aggressive or harmful. If a referee or fellow players agree that force was excessive, it is treated as an automatic foul.

Officiating

One referee calls fouls, validates legal chops, and tracks the official score. Players are responsible for tracking their own chop history; disputes are resolved by the referee.

Weight / size classes

Optional, recommended for open tournaments: Light, Mid, Heavy.

Recommended protective gear

NO!

Growing the sport

How KCG spreads.

  • Backyard league

    Start within friend groups and keep a running, informal leaderboard. The "you can never leave the game" rule naturally creates a viral, self-perpetuating player base.

  • Campus & school clubs

    KCG requires no equipment and very little space, making it easy to run as a weekly club activity with a simple ranking ladder.

  • Content strategy

    Short-form video — dramatic blocks, surprise multi-person chop chains, and penalty enforcement moments — is well-suited to social platforms and could help the game spread organically.
